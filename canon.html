The canon & library

foundational texts

The other pages try not to lean on famous names. This one is the shelf: the books, papers, and essays I actually reread. They sit behind the notebook, not in front of it.

How the canon synthesizes in practice

decision lens

01 · Economics & Frugality

Buffett + Vogels + Munger: price every byte, tie tail-latency to unit margin, and invert against ruin first.

02 · Distributed Resilience

Dynamo + Hintjens + Sagas: smart endpoints, decentralized compensations, and eventual consistency over locks.

03 · Architecture & Trade-offs

Richards & Ford + Fowler + Tanenbaum: modularity before distribution, ADR falsification, and kernel isolation.

04 · Types & Reactive State

Wlaschin + MirageOS + Async FRP: domain models as executable types and time-varying signal flows without drift.

05 · Mechanical Sympathy

Shaw + Turing + Sipser + Aho: linear memory, stack layouts, computability bounds, and respect for the hardware.

06 · Poetics & Subcultures

Wilson + Gioia + Schreiber + Newport: craft gathers in pools; hand-written code concentrates where thinking happens.

I · Economics, frugality & capital allocation

  • Poor Charlie's Almanack

    Charlie Munger

    Inversion, multidisciplinary mental models, and avoiding systemic ruin before optimizing the upside.

  • The Essays of Warren Buffett

    Warren Buffett & Lawrence Cunningham

    Circle of competence, owner earnings, and treating software architecture as capital allocation.

  • The Frugal Architect

    Werner Vogels

    Cost and tail-latency as first-class non-functional requirements tied directly to unit economics; unobserved systems lead to unknown costs.

  • Slow Productivity & Deep Work

    Cal Newport

    Do fewer things, guard cognitive depth, and let unhurried craft compound without frantic churn.

II · Distributed systems, resilience & networks

  • The ZeroMQ Guide

    Pieter Hintjens

    Lock-free concurrency, asynchronous topologies, and smart endpoints over brittle centralized brokers.

  • Dynamo: Amazon's Key-Value Store

    DeCandia et al.

    Quorums, eventual consistency, and designing for partial failure across partitioned, asynchronous networks.

  • Sagas

    Hector Garcia-Molina & Kenneth Salem (1987)

    Decomposing long-lived transactions into sequences of discrete actions paired with backward compensations without distributed locks.

III · Software architecture, modularity & trade-offs

  • Fundamentals of Software Architecture

    Mark Richards & Neal Ford

    The First & Second Laws of Software Architecture, fitness functions, and turning vague preferences into explicit trade-off rubrics.

  • Software Architecture: The Hard Parts

    Neal Ford, Mark Richards, Pramod Sadalage & Zhamak Dehghani

    Modern distributed trade-off analysis: architectural quanta, granularity vs. coupling, and choosing modular monoliths before splitting services.

  • Patterns of Enterprise Application Architecture

    Martin Fowler

    Service Layers, Domain Models, Data Mappers, and enduring structural boundaries that prevent "big ball of mud" coupling.

  • Modern Operating Systems

    Andrew S. Tanenbaum

    Clean abstraction layering and kernel-style fault isolation: peripheral failures must never compromise core invariants.

IV · Functional systems, types & reactive state

  • Domain Modeling Made Functional

    Scott Wlaschin

    Algebraic data types and railway-oriented workflows that make illegal business states unrepresentable.

  • Programming Unikernels in the Large via Functor Driven Development

    Experience Report · MirageOS

    OCaml functor parameterization to decouple core domain logic entirely from I/O drivers and storage, foundational to Ariadne.

  • Asynchronous Functional Reactive Programming for GUIs

    Evan Czaplicki & Stephen Chong

    Time-varying signals and deadlock-free reactive updates without callback cascades or client-side state divergence.

V · Mechanical sympathy, computation & compilers

  • Learn C the Hard Way

    Zed A. Shaw

    The "Cartesian dream of C": linear memory, pointer mechanics, stack/heap layouts, and raw hardware sympathy.

  • Introduction to the Theory of Computation

    Michael Sipser

    Automata, decidability, and formal theoretical bounds on what systems can compute.

  • Discourse on the Method

    René Descartes

    Methodological doubt and decomposing complex architectures into clear, verifiable constituent parts.

  • Computing Machinery and Intelligence

    Alan Turing

    Rigorous inquiry tempered by intellectual honesty about what remains ahead.

  • Compilers: Principles, Techniques & Tools

    Aho, Lam, Sethi & Ullman

    Lexing, parsing, type checking, and how high-level abstractions materialize into physical machine execution.

VI · Poetics, criticism & letters

  • Is Verse a Dying Technique?

    Edmund Wilson

    How prose absorbed narrative and history while verse narrowed into a specialized lyric craft.

  • Who Killed Poetry?

    Joseph Epstein

    A sharp warning against institutional overproduction detached from living readers.

  • Can Poetry Matter?

    Dana Gioia

    Critical honesty, loyalty to the reader, and art thriving inside a committed subculture.

  • Poetry and the Problem of Standards

    Jan Schreiber

    Why retrospective resonance resists mechanical rubrics, and how thoughtful communities collect in pools.

  • Death to the Death of Poetry

    Donald Hall

    Separating public perception from the quiet vitality of actual readers and writers.
